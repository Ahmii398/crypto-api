- **One price feed.** The research uses HistData bid prices (Dukascopy for Feb-Jul 2023). TradingView's OANDA feed differs by small amounts on every bar, so individual TradingView trades differ from the Python trades even though the rules are identical.
- **Costs are a constant percentage per fill.** Real spreads widen around news, the daily rollover and thin hours, so the short timeframes are probably treated too kindly. Overnight financing (swap) is not charged; it matters most for the 2h, 4h and 1D trades that are held for days.
- **Order of stop and target inside one bar is assumed**, using TradingView's rule (the extreme nearer the open is hit first). It was not re-checked against 1-minute data.
- **No economic calendar.** "NFP day" is the first Friday of the month and "release shock" is derived from price behaviour at 08:30 and 14:00 New York time. Other events (FOMC decisions, CPI dates as such) are not labelled.
- **Daily timeframe: too few trades.** Almost no parameter set reaches the minimum number of trades on 1D, so nothing can be concluded there in either direction.
- **The search is a sample, not exhaustive.** 5,000 random draws plus grids cover a small part of the parameter space. A better-looking set surely exists, but since in-sample rank says nothing about the next period before costs, searching harder would mostly find luckier back-tests.
- **Two-sided strategies only were put forward.** Long-only settings on gold looked better in 2010-2021 and were excluded on purpose because that is the bull market, not the entry rules. They were not evaluated out of sample.
- **PineTS is not TradingView.** The trade-for-trade parity check runs the Pine source in an open-source runtime on the research data. TradingView itself was used to compile the scripts and to record Strategy Tester results on its own data (section 11).
- **Exits are a fixed stop and a fixed target.** Break-even moves, trailing stops, partial exits and time exits were not implemented or tested.
